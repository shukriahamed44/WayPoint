01

Planning is fragmented

Orders, vehicle capacity, constraints, and store requirements live across spreadsheets, calls, and dispatcher knowledge.

What it looks like today Kavindi knows which outlets can't take a truck after eight and which mall shuts its dock at one. The spreadsheet doesn't. It also doesn't know VEH031 is in the workshop.
How Waypoint answers it One plan holds orders, capacity, cold chain and dock windows. When trucks run short, a written fairness rule suggests who waits and the dispatcher makes the call.

02

Decisions don't propagate

When a load changes, the driver, store, loader, and dispatcher may not see the same operational state.

What it looks like today Dispatch changes the stop order at 04:10. Ruwan finds out when the driver argues with him at the truck, holding a printout that is already wrong.
How Waypoint answers it Every change is an event that reaches every role: same fact, same number, same words. A printout that has gone stale is marked, and every deferral carries its reason.

03

Problems are discovered too late

Shortages, connectivity failures, delivery issues, and deferrals often surface at the point of delivery instead of where they can still be managed.

What it looks like today Four cartons missing at the dock at 05:38 become an argument at the Kegalle store at 08:44, between two people who never had the goods.
How Waypoint answers it Problems are caught where they can still be managed: a shortfall at the dock before the truck leaves, a delivery proved on the phone even with no signal.
